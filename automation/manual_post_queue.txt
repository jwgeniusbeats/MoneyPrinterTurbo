=== Why you remember insults but forget compliments: the psychology of negativity bias ===

Caption:
Have you ever wondered why a single insult can ruin your whole day, while ten compliments get forgotten in minutes?

Blame your brain's negativity bias. Millions of years ago, ignoring a threat meant death, but missing a reward just meant missing a snack.

Your brain evolved to treat criticism as an immediate danger, triggering a surge of stress hormones that lock those painful words into your long-term memory. Compliments, on the other hand, feel safe, so your brain simply lets them slip away.

You aren't crazy; you're just wired for ancient survival.

Caption:
Ever sworn Luke Skywalker said Luke, I am your father, or that the Monopoly man wore a monocle?

You have experienced the Mandela Effect, where millions share the exact same false memory.

Your brain isn't a video camera; it is a creative reconstruction engine. Every time you recall an event, your mind rebuilds it, filling in missing details with logic, pop culture, and outside influence.

This process, known as confabulation, subtly rewrites your past and convinces you it is absolute truth. You aren't crazy; your brain is just an overeager storyteller.
